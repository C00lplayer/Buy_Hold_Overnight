Plain nightly overnight: +308% over 16.5y, deep DDs

Overnight dip filter: +88.05%, Sharpe 1.86, win 58.2%, mean 0.0724%/trade
The dip filter roughly triples the gate variant's cumulative return while trading a similar fraction of nights, and it nearly doubles the per-trade edge (7.2 bps vs 2.7 bps). Economically this makes sense: buying the overnight after a within-uptrend pullback concentrates exposure on rebound nights, where the overnight drift is strongest — and it naturally avoids the euphoric-trend nights that tend to gap down. The trade-off remains the same as every overnight-only version: 7.2 bps/night is good but the strategy only captures 21.6% of nights, so absolute return (+88%) stays far below buy & hold (+779%) even though risk-adjusted quality is much higher.

The known caveats carry over: fee drag is small here (~5.7% total, the lowest of all variants), and the flat-equity/margin-usage/benchmark-Sharpe notices in the analysis are the usual artifacts of a mostly-cash book, not issues.

If you want to push further, the natural next knobs are: relaxing the down-close requirement (it's likely the most restrictive leg), requiring the pullback to be of a minimum depth (e.g. close >1% under the 5d EMA) instead of any touch, or checking whether dip nights following consecutive down days have an even stronger edge.

NOTE about 2 Sharpes: A Sharpe computed only on the 896 trade nights treats the strategy as if it's always exposed, while a Sharpe on the daily equity curve averages in the ~78% of days earning zero. The latter scales down by sqrt(exposure), which turns 1.6-1.9 into ~0.7-0.75. SPY's full daily Sharpe (0.86) is genuinely a bit higher on a calendar-day basis — it stays invested through compounding bull years, while the strategy forfeits those days. Where the strategy wins is risk: same ballpark Sharpe with a third of the drawdown (-7.9% vs -30.0%) and a much smoother curve, visible in both charts.
