The dip filter roughly triples the gate variant's cumulative return while trading a similar fraction of nights, and it nearly doubles the per-trade edge (7.2 bps vs 2.7 bps). Economically this makes sense: buying the overnight after a within-uptrend pullback concentrates exposure on rebound nights, where the overnight drift is strongest — and it naturally avoids the euphoric-trend nights that tend to gap down. The trade-off remains the same as every overnight-only version: 7.2 bps/night is good but the strategy only captures 21.6% of nights, so absolute return (+88%) stays far below buy & hold (+779%) even though risk-adjusted quality is much higher.

The known caveats carry over: fee drag is small here (~5.7% total, the lowest of all variants), and the flat-equity/margin-usage/benchmark-Sharpe notices in the analysis are the usual artifacts of a mostly-cash book, not issues.

Good question — the three numbers measure the same edge over different sampling conventions:

Engine Sharpe = 0.164 (QC statistics table)
Computed on the end-of-day equity curve, one point per calendar day over the full 16.5-year window (6,050 points).
The strategy only holds SPY on 900 of ~4,160 trading nights (22% exposure), so ~78% of daily observations are pure zero-or-tiny-cash-interest moves. All those zero days inflate the denominator (standard deviation collapses to 0.038 annualized) while the mean return is also diluted, and the annualization uses the full calendar-day count. Net effect: the reported 3.614% annual return against a tiny annualized vol gives 0.164. This number understates the risk-adjusted quality of the trades themselves — it's really a "part-time strategy" Sharpe.
In-code daily Sharpe = 0.80
Computed from my 15:59 ET equity snapshots: return from one decision-day snapshot to the next (n = 4,159 daily returns, trading days only, not calendar days).
Includes everything: trade P&L on dip nights, zero days when flat, fees, and cash interest. It excludes weekends/holidays from the sample, and the snapshot timing (15:59, before entry) aligns the return measurement with the strategy's actual overnight exposure window.
Annualized with sqrt(252) trading days: 0.0146% mean / 0.29% daily std x 15.87 ≈ 0.80. This is the most honest "what you lived through" number.
Per-trade Sharpe = 1.87
Computed only on the 897 nights when the strategy actually held SPY: the overnight return from entry fill (15:59) to exit fill (next open), frictionless.
All non-trading days are dropped entirely. Annualization assumes the strategy were invested every trading day (sqrt(252)), which is a standard way to compare an intermittent strategy's edge to a fully-invested one — it answers "how good is each bet," not "how much money did the schedule make."
How they relate

All three describe the same underlying edge; they differ by which days are in the sample and what the annualization factor assumes.
Rough consistency check: per-trade mean 0.0729% with ~21.6% of nights traded gives ~0.0157%/trading-day contribution, close to the observed 0.0146% daily mean (difference is fees and the interest on flat days). The per-trade Sharpe scales down to a daily equivalent of 1.87 x sqrt(0.216) ≈ 0.87, right in line with the measured 0.80.
The engine's 0.164 is lower still mainly because it annualizes over calendar days (365-based, diluting by weekends) and marks at end-of-day rather than the 15:59/open window.
If you want a single number for comparing to other strategies, the per-trade Sharpe (1.87, t ≈ 3.5) measures the edge quality; the in-code daily Sharpe (0.80) measures the realized risk-adjusted return of running it as specified.
