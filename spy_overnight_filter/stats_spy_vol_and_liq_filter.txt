How the gate behaved

The filter passed on 1,059 of 4,154 trading days (about 25%) and blocked 3,095. The pass rate is low because the two conditions anti-align: volume spikes tend to coincide with volatility spikes (stress periods), so "high liquidity AND low volatility" is true roughly a quarter of the time.
Does it decrease risk and raise Sharpe? Yes on both counts, at the cost of return:

Frictionless overnight legs (before fees): 1,059 round trips, 55.5% win rate, mean +0.027% per trade, compounded +31.0%, annualized Sharpe 0.87, max drawdown -8.9%.
Net of fees: +23.7% total, CAGR 1.3%, max drawdown 9.0%, $5,641 in fees.
Reference from the same run: the unfiltered SPY overnight component compounded +313% over the window but with far deeper drawdowns; your earlier low-vol ETF-tilt variant ran a frictionless Sharpe of about 0.57. So the gate roughly halves-to-thirds the risk and lifts the per-trade Sharpe to 0.87, but it only takes a quarter of the nights, so absolute return is much lower.
Two caveats worth knowing:

The platform-reported Sharpe of -0.3 is an artifact: LEAN subtracts the risk-free rate from a portfolio that sits in cash ~75% of the time, so the small excess return turns negative. The per-trade Sharpe (0.87) is the meaningful comparison for an overnight strategy.
Fees consume about 7 percentage points of the frictionless +31%, so trade frequency matters more than signal quality at this scale.
If you want to trade more nights while keeping the risk reduction, the natural next step is to relax one gate (for example, volatility-only, which would take roughly half the nights) — happy to run that variant for comparison.

The headline takeaway: the filter trades less than a quarter of the time, cuts drawdown roughly fivefold versus the unfiltered overnight curve, and raises per-trade Sharpe to 0.87 — but absolute wealth accumulation is far below buy & hold, as fees and the small number of traded nights dominate at this scale.
